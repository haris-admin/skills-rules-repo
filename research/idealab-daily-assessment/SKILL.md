# IdeaLab daily assessment

## When to Use

- The daily `IdeaLab daily assessment` cron runs (05:30 Australia/Sydney) and must feed the 06:00 briefing.
- Any manual refresh of `idealab-assessment-*.md` / `idealab-brief-*.md` or the `<!-- IDEALAB_START -->` block.

Produces the `### 💡 IdeaLab Assessment — YYYY-MM-DD` block that sits inside Pluto's 06:00 Australia/Sydney briefing. Research only: no outreach, spend, publishing or product changes.

## 1. Collect (bounded, never evade limits)

```bash
~/.hermes/venv/bin/python ~/.hermes/scripts/idealab_collect_ideas.py \
  --output-dir ~/.hermes/research_outputs/idealab \
  --data-dir ~/.hermes/data/idealab --lens all
```

- `--details ID1,ID2,...` fetches up to **10** full briefs (numeric IDs only); `--pages {1,2}`.
- The response carries `allowance`: briefs 10/day, searches 50/day, `resetsAt` ≈ 22:17 Sydney. Read it before fetching briefs.
- **The brief cap is per visitor session, not per day.** `allowance.briefs.used` equals the cumulative `opened-briefs` calls in `data/idealab/idealab-requests.jsonl`, and it does **not** reset when `resetsAt` rolls the search window over (verified 25 Oct: counter stayed 10/10 at 05:31 with searches back at 1/50). Once 10 unique briefs are open, **no new brief is obtainable in that session** — report the cap and escalate the access decision to Haris (accept summary-only / named account / retire the brief stage). Do not reset cookies or change identity to regain it.
- To read the allowance without writing files, import the module in a scratch script and make one `limit:5` `/api/idea-search` call through `Client`, then print `client.allowance`. There is no `--allowance` flag.
- **Prove the cap INSIDE one run with two allowance readings a few minutes apart — it beats comparing across days.** 9 Nov: the collector at 05:30:25 AEDT read `searches 8/50, resetsAt 2026-11-08T18:31:05Z, briefs 10/10 remaining 0`; a probe at 05:33 read `searches 1/50, resetsAt 2026-11-09T18:31:23Z, briefs 10/10 remaining 0`. The search window rolled over — 50 searches released — and released **zero** briefs. Cheap (one search), self-contained, and it needs no history.
- HTTP 401/403/429 is **terminal for the run** — report the exact refusal and coverage. Never reset cookies, rotate identity, or retry around a quota.
- The site is an undocumented visitor interface: fail visibly, never reconstruct data from previews or general news as if it were the search result.

## 2. Coverage is a sample — the register is the truth

- **Identical queries return different rows between passes.** One page per lens gives ~100 rows and is *not* stable; use `--pages 2` when you need stable daily coverage.
- A record absent from a later result set is a **ranking artefact, not a withdrawal**. `idea-register.json` (all IDs ever seen, with `content_hash`, `first_seen`, `last_surfaced_*`) is the source of truth; `candidates-YYYY-MM-DD.json` is only this pass's sample.
- Full briefs expose `record.createdAt` / `updatedAt` and `idea.updatedAt`. **Keep the field meaning**: say "record created 9 September", never "updated" when the value is `createdAt`.
- `details-YYYY-MM-DD.json` caches briefs per day; reuse them at their original `checked_at` rather than re-fetching.
- First seen ≠ newly published. The collector has no publication date; say "first seen today" and never imply a publication date.

## 3. Assess

Priority order: Buy Australian AI → AMLHive → pre-dispute → TAPEase/POS. Read titles *and* thesis from every lens, group near-duplicates, then check the shortlist (`data/idealab/shortlist.json`, max 10, `history[]` holds rejects) and `last_surfaced_hash` before re-surfacing anything.

Weights: product/customer fit 25, AU demand evidence 20, pilot in 2–4 weeks 20, revenue/distribution 15, differentiation 10, delivery/regulatory feasibility 10. Report **demand confidence separately**; never derive it from keyword counts, and never present IdeaLab market sizes, capital needs or timing as validated Australian demand.

Known overlaps to reject as "new": #1654719 (AMLHive core), #1035601 (ownership engine/C425), #979788 (C523), #813498 (C496 HiveCoach). Check `openspec/changes/` for real status (`partial`/`proposal`/`implemented`) and treat docs as planned/local evidence, not live production.

- **When a saturation test completes, publish the FUNNEL as the standing yield measure.** On 7 Nov (pass 3 met: 108 distinct, 105/108 cluster, 0 AU, 3 non-cluster all pre-rejected) the more useful output was the whole-corpus conversion: 326 records → 288 rejects (88.3%) / 17 folds (5.2%) / 14 live-watch (4.3%) / 2 defer / 1 commercial, with **0 of the 29 IDs first seen since 31 Oct carrying a live disposition** and no shortlist addition since 27 Oct. That single table answers "is this feed still producing?" for any lens without another probe. Compute it from disposition buckets, assert the buckets sum to the register total, and quote the intake window explicitly.
- **Retiring one query is the small conclusion; say the large one.** Completing the `buy_au_ai` kill test coincided with a flat distinct-ID curve (326 on 5/6/7 Nov) and intake on only 2 of the last 7 days, so the honest recommendation became *re-scope the feed* (daily → weekly pattern detector, re-run on any intake burst), not just *drop one query*. State the recommendation; never change the cron, schedule or queries yourself — that is Haris's call.
- **Cluster-vocabulary drift manufactures phantom corpus changes.** Passes 1/2/3 of the same 108-ID set reported 1 / 0 / 3 non-cluster rows purely because the token rule differed. Quote the token rule next to the number every pass (`>=2 of assurance/passport/procurement/conformity/certification/evidence/audit/attestation/trust/accredit/control plane/governance`), or a reader will read a vocabulary change as new supply.
- **Let a pre-registered expiry action expire.** #277572's "name 3 AU brokers, <=2h" check reached day 6 of 7 unstarted; the honest output is to record the kill on day 7, not to extend the clock a third time. A watch row whose only action never starts is a closed row with a calendar, and extending it is the same failure mode as re-surfacing unchanged text.

- **Report intake as CONCEPTS, not IDs — a 34-ID day can be three families and zero candidates.** 11 Nov 2026 was the largest single pass in 21 days: register 331 → 365, +34 first-seen, 0 content-hash changes. The 34 records grouped into **≈13 concepts**: 19 pre_dispute rows were one merchant dispute-evidence family, 13 tapease rows ~11 fleet-ops sub-concepts, 2 amlhive rows one "permissioned evidence workspace" (regional banks, institutional brokers). Disposition output was **0 shortlist additions**. Quote `concepts / records` next to the intake number, otherwise a family generator reads as a busy news day. **Never write an intake superlative from memory — build the `first_seen` date histogram first.** 11 Nov's "largest single-pass intake in 21 days" was false the moment it was counted: the register's bootstrap passes hold **156 (22 Oct) and 74 (23 Oct)**, and 27 and 30 Oct each had 23. The defensible phrasing was "largest non-bootstrap intake day in the register's history (34, prior max 23)". Same discipline as `total`: a comparative claim is a hard assertion, so count it or drop the adjective. Also split **rotation-vs-yesterday** from **first_seen**: same pass, `buy_au_ai` showed 23/25 "new vs yesterday" with **0 first-seen** (a reshuffle inside its 113-row set), while the real intake sat entirely in the other three lenses — check `lenses` on the first_seen rows before writing the paragraph.
- **Before rejecting a cluster as "crowded", name an Australian competitor and its price — one search does it.** 11 Nov 2026 killed the 16-row dispute-evidence family with three verified AU offers: ChargebackAU (Shopify app, launched June 2026, **USD 39/89/179 per month**), ChargeMate (**20% success fee**, names Tyro/PayWay/CommWeb/eWAY/Windcave), and Riskified Dispute Resolve marketing to AU merchants. A named AU competitor with a price and a supported processor list is a differentiation measurement; "well-funded global category" is an assumption. Query shape: `<buyer> <function> Australia providers`.
- **For any charge-reconciliation idea, read the BILLER's own account tier first — it usually already ships the data.** TollProof (#1789448) died on one fetch: linkt.com.au's taxi/fleet page (HTTP 200, 11 Nov 2026) shows the Commercial Account — Linkt's tier for "Large taxi fleets" — includes *bulk vehicle and tag management, electronic monthly invoicing and detailed trip data*, *online data & analytics — reporting and itemised accounting*, for a **$71.50/year** account fee; and TollTally (MapUp, on the Geotab marketplace for AU/NZ) already sells GPS-derived trip-level toll costs marketed as ending "driver reimbursement disputes". Order of operations for fleet-charge rows: biller's account/product page → telematics marketplace listings → only then score differentiation. The recurring pattern now has four variants (#814027 terminal rental, #1789448 tolls, #1776168 job costing, #1822525 mixed-use tax) and all are **fleet-ops features, never standalone subscriptions**.
- **Probe `site_total` is the coverage number to quote next to the register size.** Obligation-first probes on 11 Nov returned `total 42` (taxi/point-to-point) and **`total 213`** (AU merchant dispute) while the register held 365 — proof the register is a slice and "exhausted" must never be said of the catalogue. Same probes: 40 distinct rows, **24 (60%) never returned by the four lens queries in 21 days**, and **0 AU tokens in both** despite the strings literally naming "Australian" — the AU-token zero is now confirmed from a fourth direction.

- **Measure the lens geography every run**

Compute, over the union of all `candidates-*.json` files (dedupe by ID, keep lens tags), how many records in each lens mention Australia in title+thesis. Report the rate per lens in the assessment. Rationale: the priority lens can be **structurally mislabelled**. On 27 Oct the `buy_au_ai` lens was **0/81 unique records over six days** while `amlhive` was 4/65 and the rest ~1.5–2%. That is a query-weighting artefact, not an empty corpus.

- **The AU-token rate is a STYLE statistic, not a geography measure — always cross-tab it against an ICP token count.** On 9 Nov the obligation-first AUSTRAC/real-estate probe returned **17/20 ICP matches and 0/20 "Australia"/"Australian" tokens**, its top hit being our own shortlisted AU-targeted row #1496359, whose thesis describes "designated broker supervisory liability" with no geography word at all; the AU tax/trust control in the same window returned 8/20 AU. The corpus writes jurisdiction as domain terminology, so a zero AU count is fully consistent with an on-ICP page. Report the **pair** (ICP matches, AU tokens) plus the control, and never quote "0/20 AU" as "not Australian" — that inference was repeated for two weeks on this metric. Take geography from Australian primary sources (registers, scheme data, customers).
- **The AU-token count is not stable across *identical* queries — a zero is not a measurement, not even an unstable one you can trend.** Same probe string, one day apart (9 → 10 Nov 2026): `17/20 ICP + 0/20 AU` then `12/20 ICP + 4/20 AU`, with the control seam moving 8/20 → 5/20 (against 21/25 on 28 Oct and 14/25 on 29 Oct). Quote the pair with its date and never diff two days' AU counts as change; geography only from Australian primary sources.

- Test it with **1–2 probe searches** (searches are cheap, briefs are not): import the module, `Client(Path(data_dir))`, call `/api/idea-search` with an obligation-first query (`"AUSTRAC AML/CTF compliance software for Australian real estate agencies, CDD and SMR"`, `"Australian small business invoicing and payments, GST BAS reconciliation"`) and count AU mentions. Same corpus returns AU rows immediately (3–5 of 20).
- Diagnosis to report: a query built from *"Australian" + assurance-evidence/supplier-verification/procurement* tokens is drowned by the global AI-assurance cluster; the single geography token loses to four domain tokens.
- **Quantified on 28 Oct (reuse these as the baseline):** shipped `buy_au_ai` query 0/25 AU (0/81 seven-day union); the same assurance vocabulary with the word "Australian" stated **twice** still 0/10 AU; obligation-first AU tax/trust wording **21/25 (84%)**. Conclusion to state: the *domain vocabulary* controls the result, adding geography words does nothing — the fix is deletion, not emphasis.
- **A zero-AU lens is not fixable by query wording — measured and closed (1 Nov 2026).** The "rewrite the standing queries with obligation-first Australian vocabulary" plan was tested and **withdrawn**: the proposed `buy_au_ai` string named "Australian", the Privacy Principles and data sovereignty and still returned **0/20 AU** (same global assurance cluster), while the proposed `amlhive` string returned **15/20 AU but AU∩AML only 1/20** — the density was the AU tax/trust/property-reform seam, off all four lanes. The two levers trade against each other: obligation-first vocabulary buys geography, domain vocabulary buys relevance, never both. So do **not** propose a query rewrite as the fix for AU coverage again; state that the 20% AU-demand weight is a constant penalty and that Australian demand must come from Australian sources (customers, AUSTRAC/ASIC registers, scheme data).
- **Always run a control probe in the same window when a probe returns 0 AU.** A zero-AU probe result is only interpretable if the same session can still find AU rows at all. On 29 Oct the AU *dispute* wording returned 0/25 while the AU tax/trust control returned 14/25 (56%) minutes later, on a fresh search window — that pairing is what licenses the "this lane is not AU" conclusion. Without the control, a 0/25 is indistinguishable from a broken probe. Note the seam's *yield* is itself unstable (84% on 28 Oct, 56% on 29 Oct): quote it as direction, never as a constant.
- **Three of four lenses are now measured non-AU (29 Oct):** `buy_au_ai` 0/81 union, `pre_dispute` 0/25 probed (AFCA/ePayments Code/merchant-chargeback wording) and 1/61 union, `amlhive` 4/65, `tapease_pos` 3/68 — and the only AU-dense seam (AU tax/trust) is off all four lanes. State the consequence explicitly: the **20% Australian-demand weight is structurally non-discriminating on this source**, so rows are not scoring low because the ideas are weak in Australia but because IdeaLab is a global pattern feed. Offer the two honest options (take AU demand from AU customers/registers/scheme data, or accept the weight as a constant penalty) instead of quietly scoring rows down.
- **Cross-tab geography × domain, not just geography.** A lens can be "AU-rich" and still useless if its domain never overlaps ours. On 28 Oct a real-estate-anchored AML/CTF probe returned 9 AU rows and 4 AML rows and **0 that were both** (AU rows were tax/trust; AML rows were South Africa, Indonesia, US life insurers, CFTC, RERA) — while `amlhive`'s seven-day union was 4/65 (6.2%) AU. If AU∩domain ≈ 0, say the lens is a **competitor/pattern feed, not demand evidence**, and do not rewrite a second standing lens on one 25-row probe; measure two more runs first.
- **The corpus's only AU-dense seam is AU tax/trust/property-reform** (ATO pre-filing, trust distribution control rooms, indexed gains, bucket companies, rental reform). High AU rate, but outside all four lanes with no customer base — record it as a *query-design* insight, never as a product recommendation.
- Escape hatch for the row worth keeping: a probe-revealed AU row can be carried forward, but label its provenance (`source_note: diagnostic probe, not a lens query`) in both the assessment and `idea-register.json`.

## 4. Verify Australian claims at source

- **Terminal/POS-fleet ideas need a three-axed competitor screen, not one query.** On 30 Oct the pre-registered "≥2 AU products doing POS terminal-inventory reconciliation" check was run three ways: (1) *merchant-side* terminal-estate reconciliation against invoiced rental/fee lines → **0 AU products**, which is the axis the threshold must be applied to; (2) *acquirer/PSP-side* terminal inventory/lifecycle software → ≥5 products (DigiPay.Guru, Nexura, MicroTelecom, PaySuite, PAX MAXSTORE, Airwallex) — meaning the acquirer already owns the data the idea would reconstruct, so the moat is the merchant-side gap only; (3) *merchant fee/statement audit* → a populated **and free, PSP-commission-funded** AU category (Margin Rescue, PAY2TECH). Applying the threshold to axis 1 kept the score honest; conflating axes would have "failed" an idea on a competitor set that does not sell to our buyer. Report all three axes and name which one the threshold was tested against.
- **A fired EXTERNAL trigger is a legitimate re-surface even when `content_hash` is identical — but it must not move the score.** On 8 Nov #814027's IdeaLab text was byte-identical (0 hash changes across all four lenses) yet its pre-registered regulatory trigger had fired and been verified at source. The dedupe rules key on the IdeaLab record, so this falls outside them; handle it explicitly: label the re-surface `external_trigger_verified`, state *which layer* changed (mechanism/demand/differentiation) and leave the score alone unless the changed layer is **demand**. A regulator meeting its own deadline is mechanism evidence — it says nothing about willingness to pay, and scoring it up manufactures precision. Record the artefact in the register (`evidence_update_<date>`) so the next run does not re-open the same desk check.
- **Fee-audit economics: price the unit before believing the row.** AU terminal rental is AUD 24.75–29.50/month (Westpac "from", CommBank $29.50) ≈ **AUD 300–360/terminal/year**, with $500–505 unreturned-terminal fees. A reconciliation product therefore only works at **fleet** scale (tens–hundreds of terminals); a per-merchant subscription cannot clear the value it recovers. State the arithmetic instead of the adjective "small".
- **Then price it at 1 percentage point, because the answer decides build vs feature.** Recovered value is **linear in the mis-billing rate**: at 40 terminals (base AUD 12–14.4k/yr) 1pp = **AUD 120–144/yr**; at 120 terminals (AUD 36–43.2k) 1pp = AUD 360–432/yr. Reaching AUD 5k/yr needs ~35–42% over-billing at 40 terminals and ~12–14% at 120. Conclusion that follows on 8 Nov: merchant-side terminal reconciliation is a **TapEase feature inside fleet operations, not a standalone subscription** — zero acquisition cost inside an existing fleet relationship, and no standalone willingness-to-pay to find. Report that as a build/no-build recommendation rather than a score change.
- **The 30 Oct 2026 publication is now VERIFIED to exist, and it excludes the line item the terminal-reconciliation case rests on.** Fetched 8 Nov 2026, all HTTP 200: NAB's transparency page links `average-merchant-service-fees-july-to-september-2026.pdf` (**read its text layer** — it is a readable PDF: all direct merchants **0.58%**, small **0.87%**, medium **0.66%**, with card-type/channel breakdowns); CommBank states it published its first quarterly MSF report for Jul–Sep on 30 Oct; ANZ Worldline publishes 30 Oct 2026. Decisive wording for the case, from ANZ Worldline: the average MSF *"reflects only part of the operational costs of accepting payments and **not hardware costs such as terminal rental or gateway fees**"*. So the mandate hands merchants a **per-tier peer-benchmark percentage** and explicitly leaves rental outside it — the merchant-side rental gap survives the regulation, while the benchmark layer is commoditised (free PSP-commission audits quote the same number). Cite the actual figures, not "a publication appeared".
- **The 30 Oct 2026 RBA publication is a benchmark, not a ledger.** Verified 30 Oct (all HTTP 200): [§8 Impact and Implementation](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/impact-and-implementation.html), [Appendix C](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/appendix-c.html), [FAQs index](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/review-of-retail-payments-regulation/2026-03/conclusions-paper/faqs/index.html). What is published is aggregate fees as a **percentage by merchant size and card type** (networks: by card type/form factor/tokenised; acquirers over **$10bn**: merchant service fees as % of transactions). The RBA republishes it "soon after". So the trigger hands merchants a peer-comparison number — exactly what free audit services already sell — and **not** an item-level ledger. Do not claim the publication enables per-merchant fee reconciliation, and note if no publication is visible on the effective date rather than assuming compliance.

- Buy Australian AI dates live at `https://www.ai.gov.au/buy-australian-ai-partnership-program` and the Stone & Chalk page `https://www.stoneandchalk.com.au/buy-australian-ai`. Re-check every run — do not hardcode cohort dates. (Oct 2026 state: EOI closed 24 Sep, cohort 5 Oct–27 Nov, concludes 27 Nov, so no application action; programme is banking/insurance/super, so AMLHive fit is partial.) The page carries **stale "Applications are open now and close on 24 September 2026" copy alongside the key-dates list** — quote the key-dates list, and note the mismatch instead of reporting the EOI as open. Selection ≠ contract, procurement approval or funding.
- AUSTRAC guidance URLs: confirm HTTP 200 with curl and cite them for the *problem*, not for willingness to pay.

## 5. Output contract

- `idealab-assessment-YYYY-MM-DD.md` — exact titles/IDs/links, original idea vs adaptation, current product/backlog overlap, AU evidence, effort/dependencies, revenue path, risks, confidence, seven-day cheap validation.
- `idealab-brief-YYYY-MM-DD.md` — at most three candidates in priority order, each with link, proposed use, why now/fit, confidence, next cheap action, plus a coverage line. If nothing qualifies: "No strong new IdeaLab matches today."
- `idealab-brief-*.md` holds the block **without** the marker lines (markers are added at insertion). Insert between `<!-- IDEALAB_START -->` / `<!-- IDEALAB_END -->` in `morning-briefing-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, **replacing** the existing block (assert each marker occurs once). Anchor for a first insert: `\n---\n📎 *Full report:` — the block goes just above the footer, leaving all `## ` sections untouched (assert the section count is unchanged afterwards). A preview copy written by integration tests holds the same block — refresh it too or it ships stale coverage.
- Update `shortlist.json` (≤10 entries, `history[]`, review_history) and set `last_surfaced_at`/`last_surfaced_hash` in the register for surfaced IDs only.

## 6. Verify before finishing

Nonempty; today's Sydney date; markers exactly once; every surfaced ID resolves (`curl -o /dev/null -w %{http_code}` on `https://idealab.ai/idea-details/<id>` → 200); each recommendation has evidence **or an explicit evidence gap** plus a next action; no other briefing section rewritten.

## Pitfalls

- **Probe mechanics (cost 3 wasted calls on 28 Oct):** import the collector module, `Client(data_dir)`, then **`c.initialise()` before any call**. `call()` rejects any path not starting with `/api/` (`ValueError: Invalid IdeaLab API path`), so pass `/api/idea-search`, not `idea-search`. The body is the full search request — `{"query": q, "sectors": [], "capital": [], "limit": N, "sort": "match", "includeTotal": True}` — a bare `{"query","limit"}` is not what the site expects. `client.allowance` is `None` until the first call returns.

- **With zero briefs but first-seen rows, disposition EVERY first-seen row** (fold / reject-with-reason / improve-existing), not just the interesting ones. That is the whole decision output of the run: it retires rows from future candidate lists. A reject is legitimate when the thesis's own first two sentences name a jurisdiction or buyer that is not ours.
- **Screen jurisdiction and ICP before anything else on the AMLHive lens.** New rows are overwhelmingly US-specific (senior care, Medicaid, state-vendor privacy, notaries, M&A law firms, condo/HOA); one read of the thesis is enough to reject, and no score should be issued. Two structural tells: (a) it sells *to suppliers/vendors of* the regulated entity — the ICP is inverted; (b) it sells confidentiality/privacy controls, not an AML/CTF obligation.
- **Read the `vehicleLabel` as a delivery-model tell.** `Founder Need`, membership, collective, cooperative, group insurance or "specialist human review" means managed service — conflicts with the SaaS-first model and can be rejected without a brief. Likewise pooled benefits distribution.
- **Report per-lens rotation, not just first-seen counts**: comparing this pass's ID sets with the previous day's file (typical: 15–22 of 25 repeat per lens) separates "register stable, sample noisy" from real change in one line. A shortlist row can fall out of the sample entirely (#1345801 did) — check its register hash before writing anything about absence.
- **Rotation can be total: 2/25 repeat (30 Oct) on the `amlhive` lens with an identical query string and zero `content_hash` changes.** When that happens, a 23-row first-seen list is *one reshuffled ranking*, not 23 publications — say so in the first paragraph, or the disposition table reads as a busy news day. A high first-seen count with 0 hash changes and 0 changed rows is a coverage measurement, not an intake.
- **Verify the insertion by stripping the marked block and diffing the rest — not by counting `## ` headings.** Heading counts can match while the seam carries extra blank lines or a spliced fragment. After any programmatic insert, remove START..END from the new file, `difflib` it against a pre-insert backup, and require "no diff" (whitespace at the seam excepted); also re-assert the block equals the brief file byte-for-byte.
- **Never write the literal marker tokens `<!-- IDEALAB_START -->` / `<!-- IDEALAB_END -->` inside the brief body.** A Files line quoting them ("inserted between `<!-- IDEALAB_START -->` / `<!-- IDEALAB_END -->`") makes `txt.count('<!-- IDEALAB_START -->') == 1` pass while the *end* marker is doubled, so the "first START to first END" replacement splices a fragment of the brief into the middle of another section (silent, plausible-looking corruption). Say "the IDEALAB start/end markers" in prose, then assert `count(START)==1 and count(END)==1` **and** that the block equals the brief file byte-for-byte. To repair: strip from the **first** START to the **last** END (`rindex`), then re-insert once and re-check the `## ` heading list against the original.
- **Verified Australian sources for taxi/workforce rows:** `safeworkaustralia.gov.au/.../psychosocial-hazards` (HTTP 200) supports the generic PCBU psychosocial duty; there is **no** taxi-specific fatigue page — `pointtopoint.nsw.gov.au/safety/driver-fatigue` and the NSW point-to-point "driving a taxi or hire car" page both return 404. Say "evidence gap" rather than implying either way.
- **An all-repeat pass (0 first-seen, 0 changed) is a real result, not a failed run** — say so plainly and do not surface a carried row to look busy. When briefs are unavailable the highest-value work on such a sample is a **thesis-level duplicate/fold screen** against the open shortlist: title+thesis only, labelled as such in `idea-register.json` (`assessment_depth: thesis_level_no_brief`), so the row stops re-appearing as a candidate. Folds should say *which* dimension they add (segment, managed-vs-SaaS, payments) rather than just "duplicate".
- **On an all-repeat pass, screen the register's *undispensed residue* first — it is the day's real deliverable.** `disposition` is only set on rows someone has already judged; on 1 Nov **60 of the 100 sampled rows had never been dispositioned** (104 of 312 register rows carried a disposition). That residue is precisely why the same rejected cluster (AI-assurance, US housing desks, lobbying desks) kept reappearing in candidate lists for eight days. Disposition every undisposed row in the sample from title+thesis alone — jurisdiction/ICP first, then delivery vehicle, then fold target — and the next passes either surface something new or short-circuit in minutes. Report the before/after disposition count (e.g. 104 → 164) as the run's output.

- **Re-read a carried row's stated BUYER from the thesis before applying a pre-registered threshold.** The 30 Oct check on #814027 tested merchant-side terminal-estate reconciliation (0 AU products) and raised differentiation to "measured, holds"; on 31 Oct the same unchanged text turned out to name *"payment acquirers, processors, and acquiring banks subject to mandatory inventory reporting"* — the acquirer side, where the same screen had found ≥5 vendors. A threshold applied to an **adaptation** yields a score the row cannot defend. Quote the buyer from the thesis first; if the check's axis differs from the row's, score the row as written and label the adaptation separately (here: differentiation 6 → 3, score 66 → 63, rank unchanged). This costs nothing when briefs are unavailable — the thesis alone carries the customer field.
- **A probe that reveals a *cluster* is a differentiation signal, not just coverage.** On 29 Oct an AU-payments/POS probe surfaced #813912, #814096, #1437142, #1123234, #1436555 — none returned by the standing `tapease_pos` query in eight days — and #813912/#814096 were further variants of #814027's TerminalLedger concept, making the family five known variants (with #1436549 already folded).
