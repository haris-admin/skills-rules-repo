# Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers Strategic Framework Guide

Hamilton Helmer's *7 Powers: The Foundations of Business Strategy* establishes that a business must possess at least one persistent differential advantage ("Power") to create long-term enterprise value. A Power requires two simultaneous conditions:
1. **Benefit**: Increases value or lowers costs for the business.
2. **Barrier**: Prevents competitors from copying the benefit.

---

## The 7 Powers Diagnostic

| Power | Benefit | Barrier | Early-Stage Startup Applicability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Counter-Positioning** | A new business model that delivers superior customer value. | An incumbent cannot replicate the model without damaging their core, profitable legacy revenue stream ("The Innovator's Dilemma"). | **Highest**. This is the primary weapon of startups against entrenched giants. |
| **2. Cornered Resource** | Exclusive access to proprietary IP, regulatory licenses, unique talent, or non-public data. | Legal protection (patents, exclusive contracts) or unique access that cannot be bought on open markets. | **High**. Critical for FinTech, regtech, and AI domains. |
| **3. Switching Costs** | Customer lifetime value and pricing power increase over time. | Customer faces massive frictional costs, workflow breakdown, or data loss if they switch to a rival. | **Medium-High**. Accrues as customers store workflow history in your tool. |
| **4. Process Power** | Unmatched quality, speed, or cost efficiency embedded in organizational routines. | Embedded company muscle memory and thousands of operational micro-optimizations that cannot be reverse-engineered. | **Medium**. Takes years of accumulated execution to solidify. |
| **5. Network Economies** | Value of the platform increases exponentially with each new user or merchant onboarded. | Multi-sided network density creates a winner-take-most barrier (e.g. 3-sided dispute networks). | **High** (if multi-sided marketplace or network). |
| **6. Scale Economies** | Lower cost-per-unit across high production or cloud compute volumes. | Capital intensity: competitors must burn immense cash to reach equivalent unit economics. | **Low initially**. Accrues at later growth stages. |
| **7. Branding** | Premium pricing and default customer trust. | Decades of positive customer experience and cultural reinforcement. | **Low initially**. Accrues gradually via authority and thought leadership. |

## Incumbent Retaliation Analysis: The "Can't vs Won't" Test

When evaluating an adversary, categorize their likely response:
1. **Can't Retaliate (Capability Barrier)**: Legacy tech architecture, technical debt, or rigid regulatory approvals prevent them from shipping modern features quickly.
2. **Won't Retaliate (Incentive Barrier / Counter-Positioning)**: Retaliating would require cannibalizing their own high-margin cash cow (e.g. big banks abandoning profitable dispute penalty fees, or consulting firms replacing \$500/hr billing with \$100/mo software).
