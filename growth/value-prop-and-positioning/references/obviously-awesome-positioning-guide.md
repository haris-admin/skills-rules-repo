# April Dunford's "Obviously Awesome" Positioning Guide

Positioning is the act of deliberately defining how you are the best at something that a specific market of customers cares a lot about. It is not messaging, branding, or tagline creation; it is the context that makes your product's value obvious to the buyer.

---

## The 5 Components of Effective Positioning

```
1. COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES
   What would customers do if your product did not exist?
               │
               ▼
2. UNIQUE CAPABILITIES / ATTRIBUTES
   What features or capabilities do you have that alternatives lack?
               │
               ▼
3. VALUE & PROOF
   What benefit do those capabilities enable for the customer?
               │
               ▼
4. TARGET CUSTOMER SEGMENT
   Who cares deeply about that specific value?
               │
               ▼
5. MARKET CATEGORY
   What frame of reference makes that value obvious?
```

---

## The 10-Step Positioning Exercise

1. **Identify your best, most enthusiastic customers.** Who loves the product, pays on time, and refers others?
2. **List their real competitive alternatives.** Include "hiring an intern", "using Excel", or "doing nothing".
3. **Isolate your differentiated capabilities.** What can you do that alternatives cannot?
4. **Translate features into tangible business value.** Not "automated database indexing", but "reduces audit preparation time from 3 days to 4 minutes".
5. **Pinpoint the target customer characteristics.** What specific attributes make a customer care desperately about this value (e.g., firms facing regulatory audits within 6 months)?
6. **Select a market category.**
   - *Head-to-Head*: Win against the established leader in an existing category (very expensive).
   - *Big Fish, Small Pond (Sub-segment)*: Dominate a specific niche within an existing category (e.g. "CRM for Dental Practices" or "Dispute Resolution for Hospitality Merchants").
   - *Create a New Category*: Define an entirely new category (high education burden, but massive long-term reward).
7. **Create a positioning statement.**
   - *For [Target Customer] who [Need/Pain], [Product] is a [Market Category] that [Key Benefit]. Unlike [Primary Alternative], our product [Primary Differentiator].*
