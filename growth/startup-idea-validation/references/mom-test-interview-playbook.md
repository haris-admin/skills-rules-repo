# The Mom Test Customer Interview Playbook

Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* framework applied to early-stage venture validation. The core premise: **People will lie to you if they think it's what you want to hear. Never ask anyone if your business is a good idea.**

---

## The Three Golden Rules

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.**
   - Focus on what they currently do, where their time and money leak, and what frustrates them today.
2. **Ask about specific past behaviors, not generic opinions or future promises.**
   - People are terrible at predicting their own future actions. What they did last week is proof; what they promise to do next month is fiction.
3. **Talk less and listen more.**
   - The founder should speak no more than 20% of the interview. Silence is your best research tool.

---

## Question Calibration Matrix

| ❌ Bad Question (Opinion / Flattery / Hypothetical) | ✅ Good Question (Fact / Past Behavior / Workaround) | Why the Good Question Works |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Do you think a platform that automates chargeback disputes is a good idea?" | "When was the last time a customer filed a chargeback against your store? Walk me through what happened next." | Anchors to a real incident, surfacing actual friction, tools used, and emotional cost. |
| "Would you pay $49/month for this software?" | "What software tools or services did you purchase or renew for your business in the last 60 days? How did you justify that spend?" | Uncovers real willingness to pay and budget allocation mechanisms. |
| "What features would you love to see in an ideal BAS filing tool?" | "How did you file your last BAS? Where did you get stuck, and what workarounds did you use?" | Reveals existing hacks (spreadsheets, paper, manual exports) that prove genuine pain. |
| "Do you think teachers would like a sensory-friendly student dashboard?" | "Can you show me what accommodations or modifications you set up for your neurodivergent students last term?" | Tests whether the educator already expends effort solving this problem today. |

## Commitment Ladder (Skin in the Game)

Complementary praise ("This sounds amazing!") is worthless. Look strictly for commitments:

1. **Time Commitment**: Agreeing to a follow-up test, providing access to workflow logs, or introducing colleagues.
2. **Reputation Commitment**: Making a warm introduction to their team lead, CFO, or peer business owner.
3. **Financial Commitment**: Paying a deposit, signing a non-binding Letter of Intent (LOI), or pre-ordering before build.
