# Bottom-Up Market Sizing Methodology Guide

A defensible market sizing model avoids top-down vanity figures (e.g. "Gartner says this is a $50B market and we will capture 1%"). Investors and strategic leaders demand **bottom-up unit economics**.

---

## 1. The Bottom-Up Core Formula

$$\text{TAM} = \text{Total Count of Target Accounts} \times \text{Annual Contract Value (ACV)}$$

$$\text{SAM} = \text{TAM} \times \text{Addressability Constraints Percentage}$$

$$\text{SOM} = \text{SAM} \times \text{Realistic 3-Year Capture Percentage}$$

---

## 2. Addressability Constraints for SAM

Never assume 100% of the TAM is reachable. Filter by real operating barriers:
1. **Geographic & Regulatory**: e.g., AU-only entities governed by ASIC/AUSTRAC or Australian consumer law.
2. **Firmographic & Technical**: e.g., merchants using POS systems that support webhooks or open APIs (Clover, Square, Stripe).
3. **Channel Capacity**: Segments accessible through direct inbound, self-serve, or existing affiliate partnerships.

---

## 3. SOM Realism Gates

SOM is constrained by execution capacity, not market appetite:
- **Sales Velocity**: $\text{Quota per Sales Rep} \times \text{Number of Reps} \times 12\text{ months}$.
- **Marketing Pipeline**: $\text{Monthly Ad/Content Spend} / \text{Blended CAC} \times \text{Close Rate}$.
- **Customer Onboarding Bandwidth**: How many reporting entities or merchants can be onboarded per month without customer success failure.
- A healthy early-stage SOM represents **2% to 10% of SAM** over 36 months. Any model claiming >20% SOM in year 1 triggers immediate investor disbelief.

---

## 4. Triangulation Checks

Always compare the bottom-up model against two external vectors:
- **Top-Down Ceiling Check**: Does your TAM exceed the entire industry spend reported by ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics) or IBISWorld? If yes, your ACV or count is inflated.
- **Value-Theory Floor Check**: How much hard money or labor hours does your product save? (e.g. saving $40 per chargeback dispute × 50 disputes/year = $2,000 value. Pricing at $960/year captures ~48% of the economic surplus created).
